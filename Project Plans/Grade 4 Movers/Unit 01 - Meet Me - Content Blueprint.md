# Grade 4 Movers — Unit 1: Meet Me!

## Unit identity

**Length:** 3 weeks  
**Core sessions:** 9 × 45–60 minutes  
**Self-study:** 6 short missions, approximately 10–15 minutes each  
**Big picture:** `assets/grade-4-movers/big-picture-scenes/01-meet-me.png`  
**Unit narrative:** The school Welcome Fair is beginning, but the mascot has forgotten the details needed for its new friend badge. Learners explore the fair, meet children, collect useful language, help the mascot introduce itself, and finally create their own spoken introduction.

The narrative is deliberately low-stakes. Its purpose is to make repeated language practice feel connected while preserving space for learners to talk about their real identities.

---

## 1. Learning outcomes

By the end of the unit, learners can:

1. Introduce themselves using their name, age, home or school context, interests, abilities, and one personal detail.
2. answer at least six familiar personal questions with a relevant sentence rather than a single word.
3. ask at least four familiar personal questions with intelligible word order.
4. extend an answer with a detail or a reason using **and**, **but**, or **because**.
5. describe what a person in a picture has, wears, likes, or is doing.
6. listen for names, ages, spellings, interests, and preferences in short conversations.
7. read and respond to a short personal profile or friendly message.
8. write a short profile of 5–7 connected sentences with appropriate capitals, spacing, and full stops.
9. use a simple communication-repair phrase when they need help.
10. record a 45–60 second introduction that is understandable and personally meaningful.

### Student-facing success criteria

- I can say more than my name and age.
- I can answer with a sentence and add a detail.
- I can ask someone a question too.
- I can say why I like or dislike something.
- I can keep going when I forget a word.
- I can listen to my recording and improve one thing.

---

## 2. Target language

### Communicative functions

- Greeting and introducing yourself
- Asking for and giving personal information
- Talking about interests and preferences
- Talking about possessions and abilities
- Giving a reason
- Showing interest in another speaker
- Asking for repetition or thinking time

### Core question–answer frames

| Function | Question | Expected answer development |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | What’s your name? | My name’s ____. / I’m ____. |
| Age | How old are you? | I’m nine years old. |
| Home | Where do you live? | I live in ____. |
| School | What class are you in? | I’m in Grade 4 / Class ____. |
| Interests | What do you like doing? | I like drawing and reading. |
| Preference | What’s your favourite ____? | My favourite ____ is ____ because ____. |
| Ability | What can you do well? | I can swim quite well. |
| Possession | Have you got a ____? | Yes, I have. / No, I haven’t. |
| Frequency | How often do you ____? | I ____ every day / at weekends / twice a week. |
| Follow-up | What about you? | I ____. |

### Grammar in context

- **be:** I am / I’m; you are / you’re; he/she is
- **have got:** I’ve got; Have you got…?; he/she has got
- **can/can’t** for ability
- **like/love/enjoy + noun or -ing** at a controlled level
- Present simple for repeated activities
- **and, but, because** to extend meaning
- Familiar question forms with **what, where, who, how old, how often**

Grammar is taught as a speaking resource. Learners first encounter it in profiles and conversations, then notice and manipulate it, and finally use it to communicate.

### Vocabulary

**Core productive set:**

- Personal information: name, age, class, school, city, birthday
- Interests: drawing, painting, reading, singing, dancing, playing football, playing basketball, playing chess, taking photos, cooking, collecting, making things
- School/community fair: badge, table, book, ball, picture, paintbrush, instrument, pet, friend, group
- Personal qualities and feelings: friendly, funny, kind, helpful, quiet, excited, nervous, proud
- Frequency: every day, after school, at weekends, sometimes, often, never

**Receptive/stretch set:**

- creative, curious, confident, practise, introduce, interest, similar, different

Target approximately **28 productive items** and **8 receptive/stretch items**. Known Starters vocabulary should be recycled rather than counted as new learning.

### Pronunciation

- Clear final consonants in **like**, **live**, **friend**, and **old**
- Contrast between **can** in statements and questions
- Sentence stress on meaningful information: “I like **DRAWING**.”
- Friendly rising intonation in yes/no questions
- Chunking an introduction into short thought groups rather than reading word by word

### Communication-repair phrases

- Could you say that again, please?
- Let me think.
- I’m not sure, but I think…
- What does ____ mean?
- How do you say ____ in English?

## 3. Three-week learning sequence

## Week 1 — Discover: Who is at the Welcome Fair?

### Session 1: Enter the fair

**Learning intention:** Notice and use language for identity, interests, and visible actions.

1. Mascot welcome and narrative hook: it needs help completing its friend badge.
2. Silent observation of the big picture: notice five details independently.
3. Pair routine: “I can see…” / “Where?” / “Near the…”
4. Guided hotspot exploration of eight major activity clusters.
5. Listen-and-find: identify characters from clothing, possessions, and activities.
6. Personal bridge: choose one fair station you would visit and explain why.
7. Exit recording: name, age, and one interest; 15–20 seconds.

**Evidence:** baseline spontaneous speaking sample; hotspot comprehension.

### Session 2: Ask and answer

**Learning intention:** Ask and answer familiar personal questions.

1. Retrieve six hotspot words from the scene.
2. Hear two children meeting at the fair.
3. Reconstruct the conversation using response-choice cards.
4. Notice question words and auxiliary order.
5. “Question carousel” with classmates or virtual character cards.
6. Add the follow-up move: “What about you?”
7. Reflect: which question was easiest or hardest to answer?

**Evidence:** four accurate questions and relevant answers.

### Session 3: Give a bigger answer

**Learning intention:** Extend an answer with a detail or reason.

1. Compare one-word, sentence, and extended responses.
2. Sort answer endings under **and**, **but**, and **because**.
3. Listen for which speaker gives the most useful answer and explain why.
4. Build “answer ladders”:
   - I like music.
   - I like music and I play the keyboard.
   - I like music because it makes me happy.
5. Describe and infer the interests of three children in the big picture.
6. Record one improved answer.

**Evidence:** at least one relevant extension without copying a complete model.

### Self-study missions, Week 1

- **Explore:** Find eight people or objects in the big picture from audio clues.
- **Remember:** Visual flashcards and listen-and-choose for core interests vocabulary.

---

## Week 2 — Practise: Find your people

### Session 4: Likes, abilities, and frequency

**Learning intention:** Explain what you like, what you can do, and how often you do it.

1. Retrieval grid: interest + action + frequency.
2. Mini listening profiles for four fair visitors.
3. Match each child to the correct station and justify the match.
4. Language focus: **like + -ing**, **can**, and frequency phrases.
5. Information-gap partner survey.
6. Create a “same, different, surprising” comparison with a partner.

**Evidence:** three connected facts and one comparison.

### Session 5: The Friend Finder mystery

**Learning intention:** Listen for personal details and identify the correct person.

1. Mascot receives incomplete clues about a possible new friend.
2. Learners listen for name spelling, age, clothing, interest, and location.
3. Select the matching person in the big picture.
4. Check evidence: “It must be ____ because…”
5. A second round includes one distractor and one corrected detail.
6. Learners create an oral clue set for another character.

**Movers connection:** Listening for names and descriptions; matching people in a busy scene.

**Evidence:** accurate detail capture and spoken justification.

### Session 6: Read and write a profile

**Learning intention:** Understand and create a short personal profile.

1. Read three illustrated profile cards for gist: choose the best station for each child.
2. Read again for details and complete a comparison table.
3. Notice capitals, full stops, pronoun consistency, and sentence order.
4. Sentence scramble: reconstruct a coherent profile.
5. Plan a real or fictional profile using keyword notes.
6. Write a 5–7 sentence first draft.
7. Peer check using “clear, connected, correct.”

**Evidence:** first writing draft.

### Self-study missions, Week 2

- **Listen:** Complete a friend card from a short conversation: name, spelling, age, interest, and favourite item.
- **Build:** Sentence scramble and question–answer matching, followed by one recorded response.

---

## Week 3 — Perform: Welcome to my world

### Session 7: Speak clearly and keep going

**Learning intention:** Improve clarity, fluency, and communication repair.

1. Listen to contrasting recordings: rushed, word-by-word, and well chunked.
2. Mark thought groups in a short model introduction.
3. Practise final sounds and sentence stress.
4. Mascot role-play deliberately asks for repetition.
5. Learners practise a repair phrase and continue speaking.
6. Record–listen–improve cycle using one 30-second introduction.

**Evidence:** one identifiable improvement between recordings.

### Session 8: Welcome Fair interview

**Learning intention:** Sustain a two-way conversation.

1. Prepare keywords only—no full script.
2. Interview in pairs or with the mascot:
   - greeting
   - three personal questions
   - two follow-up questions
   - one similarity or difference
   - friendly closing
3. Switch roles.
4. Listener reports one memorable fact about the speaker.
5. Self-assess interaction and answer development.

**Evidence:** approximately 90 seconds of two-way interaction.

### Session 9: My introduction showcase

**Learning intention:** Produce, review, and celebrate an independent spoken introduction.

1. Quick retrieval warm-up without notes.
2. Final preparation using a six-icon prompt card.
3. Record a 45–60 second personal introduction.
4. Answer two unseen but familiar follow-up questions.
5. Publish the revised written profile beside the recording.
6. Compare the final recording with the Session 1 baseline.
7. Choose one Unit 2 speaking goal.

**Evidence:** final speaking performance, follow-up responses, revised writing, and reflection.

### Self-study missions, Week 3

- **Rehearse:** Mascot interview with optional question replay and graduated support.
- **Showcase:** Final recording, self-review, and one re-record opportunity before submission.

## 4. Big-picture interactive content

### Proposed hotspot clusters

| Hotspot | Visual cluster | Language purpose | Interaction |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Football child | interests, possessions | Audio clue + personal question |
| H2 | Painter and easel | colors, activities, preference | Dialogue + “What do you like making?” |
| H3 | Two readers | books, favourites, comparison | Information card + follow-up question |
| H4 | Child showing pet picture | pets, have got, feelings | Short listening question |
| H5 | Music pair | instruments, can/can’t | Audio + choice + personal response |
| H6 | Badge-making table | names, spelling, colors | Listen for spelling + find item |
| H7 | Shy child welcomed by peers | feelings and social language | Inference question + supportive response |
| H8 | Mascot | unit narrative and reflection | Branching dialogue |

### Exploration rounds

1. **Free discovery:** Tap any hotspot and hear a short character introduction.
2. **Listen and find:** Locate a person from two or three details.
3. **Who could it be?:** Use inference; more than one answer may be acceptable with a reason.
4. **Connect to me:** Answer a personal question linked to each station.
5. **Memory return:** Revisit the scene later without highlights and retrieve character details.

### Example hotspot language

**Painter:** “Hi! I’m Mia. I love painting pictures of the sea. I paint after school on Fridays. What do you like making?”

**Football child:** “This boy has got a black-and-white ball. He’s standing beside the art table. He likes playing outside. Can you find him?”

**Welcoming group:** “One child looks nervous. How do you know? What could you say to help them join the group?”

## 6. Core input texts

### Model dialogue: first meeting

> **A:** Hi! I’m Sam. What’s your name?  
> **B:** I’m Linh. Nice to meet you.  
> **A:** Nice to meet you too. What do you like doing?  
> **B:** I like drawing animals because they’re interesting. What about you?  
> **A:** I love playing basketball. I play after school twice a week.  
> **B:** Cool! Can you play very well?  
> **A:** I’m not sure, but I’m getting better!

### Model profile: core level

> My name is Linh and I’m nine years old. I live with my family in Ho Chi Minh City. I like drawing and reading comics. My favourite animals are dolphins because they are clever. I can swim, but I can’t swim very fast. At weekends, I often draw pictures with my cousin.

### Stretch profile

> Hi, I’m Arun. I’m ten and I’m a curious person. I enjoy building models because I like finding out how things work. I’m good at science, but spelling is sometimes difficult for me. After school, I usually play chess with my grandfather. I’d like to meet someone who enjoys making things too.

All final profiles should be checked against the selected Movers vocabulary and grammar boundaries before release.

## 7. Assessment design

### Baseline speaking sample

Prompt: “Tell us your name, your age, and something you like.”  
Maximum: 20 seconds.  
Purpose: comparison and personalization, not grading.

### Final performance

**Part A — Prepared introduction, 45–60 seconds**

Learners should cover at least five of these six areas:

1. name and age
2. home or school
3. interests
4. favourite thing and reason
5. ability
6. routine or frequency

**Part B — Two follow-up questions**

Questions are selected from a familiar bank but not shown before recording.

### Student-friendly speaking rubric

| Dimension | 1 — Starting | 2 — Developing | 3 — Secure | 4 — Strong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Message | Gives isolated information | Gives several relevant facts | Communicates a clear connected introduction | Adds memorable detail and reasons |
| Language | Relies heavily on prompts | Uses short familiar sentences | Uses suitable questions and linked sentences | Uses varied familiar language with control |
| Clarity | Often difficult to understand | Understandable with listener effort | Generally clear and appropriately paced | Clear, well chunked, and confident |
| Interaction | Needs questions repeated or simplified | Answers familiar questions | Answers and asks or follows up | Responds naturally and repairs difficulty |

Accuracy matters, but comprehensible independent communication has priority. A learner should not lose the sense of success because of a few developmentally appropriate errors.

### Objective mini-check

- 4 listening questions
- 3 reading/dialogue questions
- 3 vocabulary/grammar questions
- 2 short written responses
- Speaking assessed separately through the performance

### Evidence stored for later personalization

- Can answer with a full sentence
- Can extend with a reason/detail
- Can form familiar questions
- Can retrieve target interests vocabulary
- Can understand names/descriptions
- Can use a repair phrase
- Pronunciation or clarity priority
- Confidence level from learner reflection

## 8. Differentiation

### Support available on demand

- Replay and slower replay
- Picture vocabulary bank
- Sentence starters rather than complete scripts
- Question icons
- Rehearsal before recording
- Mascot models one response
- Choice between two useful linking words
- Reduced final target: 30 seconds and four content areas

### Core expectation

- Keyword notes only
- 45–60 second introduction
- At least one reason or extra detail
- Two follow-up responses

### Stretch

- Explain two preferences
- Include a contrast with **but**
- Ask the listener two questions
- Compare yourself with someone in the scene
- Respond to an unexpected familiar follow-up
- Speak from icon prompts without written sentences

Support should fade across the unit. Learners may select support without being publicly assigned a level.

---

## 9. Safeguarding and identity choices

- Learners may use a first name, nickname, avatar name, or fictional profile.
- Do not request an address, phone number, exact birth date, school location, or other identifying information.
- “Where do you live?” should accept a city, region, or country-level answer.
- Learners do not need to disclose family circumstances, religion, medical information, or possessions.
- Personal recordings should remain visible only within the authorized student–teacher workflow.
- Alternatives must be available for learners who are not ready to record their real identity.

## 10. Production order

### Sprint A — Unit spine

1. Copy the big picture into the student media library.
2. Build the opening story and mascot narrative.
3. Configure the eight hotspot clusters.
4. Write and record hotspot audio.
5. Build the baseline speaking prompt.

### Sprint B — Input and controlled practice

6. Produce the four model character profiles.
7. Build the interest vocabulary pack.
8. Build listen-and-find, best-response, question scramble, and answer ladder activities.
9. Produce the Friend Finder listening mission.

### Sprint C — Reading, writing, and performance

10. Produce three reading profiles and questions.
11. Build the profile planner and revision checklist.
12. Produce pronunciation models.
13. Build the mascot interview and speaking recordings.
14. Build the mini-check and learner reflection.

### Sprint D — Quality assurance

15. Verify language against Movers boundaries.
16. Test every activity on student devices.
17. Check audio clarity, keyboard access, alt text, and hotspot target sizes.
18. Test supported, core, and stretch routes with representative learners.
19. Review whether the total workload fits three weeks.
20. Release, observe, and revise before scaling the pattern to Unit 2.

---

## 11. Definition of done

Unit 1 is complete when:

- Every activity supports at least one stated learning outcome.
- Speaking occurs in all nine sessions and at least three self-study missions.
- The big picture is reused for discovery, listening, reasoning, and personal application.
- Learners encounter core language several times across different skills.
- There is a baseline and final comparable speaking sample.
- Students receive one clear strength and one next step.
- Support can be accessed without labeling the learner.
- No required personal disclosure creates a safeguarding concern.
- The unit produces usable mastery evidence for future review.
- The final experience feels like a connected Welcome Fair journey rather than a quiz collection.
