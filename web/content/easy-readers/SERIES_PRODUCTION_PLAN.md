# WeKnow English Easy Readers — production plan

## Purpose

The series helps A1 learners build reading confidence through short, highly illustrated stories. Each book combines a clear narrative, controlled language, contextual vocabulary, listening support, one retrieval check per chapter, and a short speaking prompt.

The main learner is a primary-age English student. Teachers need predictable learning targets and checks; parents need visible progress; administrators need a repeatable production and quality-assurance system.

## MVP definition: Book 1

**The New Student** is the reference implementation for the series.

- Level: A1
- Story length: 500–700 words
- Structure: 6 chapters
- Language: `be`, `have`, introductions, countries, hobbies, and school vocabulary
- Player: cover/start screen, chapter contents, progress, read-aloud, vocabulary support, text-size control, comprehension checks, speaking prompts, saved device progress, and completion state
- Artwork: one cover, a cast reference, and one landscape story illustration per chapter
- Assessment: six selected-response checks, one per chapter

Book 1 is considered finalized when all items below pass:

1. A teacher confirms the story, vocabulary, questions, and speaking prompts match the intended class age.
2. An A1 language audit confirms sentence length, grammar load, vocabulary support, and total word count.
3. Artwork is checked for character, clothing, setting, and object consistency.
4. At least five target-age students can navigate the reader without adult explanation.
5. At least four of five students can answer five of six checks after supported reading.
6. Read-aloud, touch controls, keyboard controls, small screens, and large text all work.
7. Any student confusion is recorded and resolved before the series template is frozen.

## Frozen series template

After Book 1 approval, freeze these reusable rules:

- Six-chapter arc: arrival/setup → people and place → small challenge → shared activity → relationship/solution → positive ending
- 80–125 story words per chapter, adjusted to the book’s approved total
- Three useful vocabulary items per chapter
- One literal comprehension check per chapter
- One personal speaking prompt per chapter
- One illustration per chapter, with no embedded text
- A recurring visual palette and fixed character sheets for recurring children
- A1 text defaults: concrete nouns, high-frequency verbs, short clauses, clear pronoun references, and minimal idiom
- New grammar appears in context but is never needed to understand every event

## Production pipeline for Books 2–10

### 1. Learning map

Lock the communicative purpose, grammar, vocabulary groups, word range, and six chapter outcomes before drafting prose.

### 2. Story draft

Write the six chapters as one complete arc. Make the problem understandable from the pictures, give each chapter a small change, and end with emotional safety and learner success.

### 3. Educational edit

Check age appropriateness, A1 readability, repetition with purpose, cultural inclusivity, factual accuracy, and whether the target language is useful rather than forced.

### 4. Activity layer

Add 18 vocabulary supports, six quick checks, six speaking prompts, illustration descriptions, accessibility text, and read-aloud text.

### 5. Art production

Create or update the cast sheet first. Generate the six chapter illustrations from approved scene briefs. Review character identity, clothing, props, time of day, geography, emotional expression, hands, accidental text, and continuity.

### 6. Player assembly

Add the book as data using the Book 1 schema. Reuse the same player; do not create a new interface for each title. Confirm metadata and the correct cover.

### 7. Quality assurance

Run automated content checks, build validation, accessibility checks, and a human page-by-page review. Pilot with students and record reading time, navigation errors, misunderstood words, and question success.

### 8. Release and improvement

Release in small waves, review learner evidence, and update the series rules only when the change improves learning across more than one title.

## Production order

| Wave | Books | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reference | 1. The New Student | Finalizes the player, language rules, and visual system |
| Wave 1 | 2. Where Is Milo?; 3. Mia’s Very Busy Morning | Reuses the core cast while testing home, neighborhood, routine, and prepositions |
| Wave 2 | 4. The School Garden; 5. The Mystery Box | Tests inquiry stories, classroom vocabulary, `can/can’t`, and question forms |
| Wave 3 | 6. A Day at the Beach; 7. The Best Birthday Ever? | Adds present continuous and a carefully scaffolded introduction to past meaning |
| Wave 4 | 8. The Little Robot; 9. The Camping Night | Tests commands, action verbs, nature language, suspense, and simple past |
| Finale | 10. Keelan Comes to Earth | Brings the series language together and connects to the wider WeKnow learning world |

## Per-book deliverables

- Approved learning map and table of contents
- Final story manuscript and word-count report
- Vocabulary list, comprehension checks, answers, and speaking prompts
- Cover, cast reference, six final illustrations, and accessibility descriptions
- Player data file using the shared schema
- Automated content tests and completed human QA checklist
- Student pilot notes, fixes, and final approval record

## Measures that matter

- Completion rate by chapter and book
- Listen-button use compared with independent reading
- Vocabulary cards opened and replayed
- First-attempt and eventual check accuracy
- Chapters where learners stop or repeatedly return
- Student ability to retell the story with pictures
- Teacher rating for level fit, engagement, and classroom usefulness

These measures should improve the next book’s teaching design. They should not be used to punish slower readers or turn recreational reading into a high-pressure test.
