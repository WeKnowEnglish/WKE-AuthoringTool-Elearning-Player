# Grade 4 Movers — Unit 1, Session 2

## Find a Fair Friend

**Status:** production plan  
**Length:** 45 minutes  
**Primary stakeholder:** Grade 4 learner  
**Cambridge Movers emphasis:** personal questions, listening for specific information, short spoken answers, and describing a person

## Session promise

Keelan's Friend Finder has three empty profile cards. The learner returns to the Welcome Fair, meets three children in any order, asks questions, listens for clues, and chooses a new fair friend to introduce. The lesson ends with a short two-turn conversation rather than another isolated recording.

This keeps the world and language from Session 1 familiar while moving the learner from talking only about themself to taking part in a reciprocal conversation.

## Progression from Session 1

| Session 1 foundation | Session 2 step forward | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `My name is ... / I'm ... / I like ...` | Understand and answer the matching questions | Connects statements to real conversation |
| Choose and describe a fair station | Ask another person about an interest | Gives the language a genuine purpose |
| Identify `He/She likes ...` in a picture | Remember a spoken detail and report it | Moves from recognition to listening and retrieval |
| Record one prepared answer | Complete a two-turn listen-and-speak exchange | Builds conversational timing and independence |
| Receive word-part clarity feedback | Receive feedback on a question chunk and one key answer word | Keeps feedback specific and non-punitive |
| Explore every station | Choose which friend to meet first | Preserves learner agency without losing progression |

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the session, the learner can:

1. understand the questions **What's your name?**, **How old are you?**, **What do you like doing?**, and **Can you ...?**;
2. ask at least two of those questions using an intelligible question chunk;
3. listen for and select a person's name, age, interest, and ability;
4. answer a personal question in a short complete sentence;
5. introduce one person using **This is ... . He/She likes ... .**

### Success criteria

- Complete at least two of three friend profiles without a model replay.
- Answer at least 2 of 3 final listening checks correctly.
- Produce one accepted question and one meaningful personal answer, with supported retry available.
- Complete the final introduction with visual sentence frames; accuracy can be supported, but meaning must be clear.

## Target language

### Retrieval language

- My name is ...
- I'm ... years old.
- I like painting / reading / playing football / making music.
- He/She likes ...
- Yes, I can. / No, I can't.

### New productive language

- What's your name?
- How old are you?
- What do you like doing?
- Can you ...?
- This is ... . He/She likes ... .

### Optional stretch

- We both like ...
- He/She can ... too.
- Nice to meet you.

The core path should not require the stretch language. It becomes a bonus star for confident learners.

## Story and guided-dialog plan

The opening should retain the Session 1 interaction grammar: full-screen scene, central play button, short voiced turns, large visual actions, full-colour Keelan, and a dimmed background with one highlighted target.

| Beat | Story action | Learner action | Language purpose | Completion evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Welcome back | Keelan notices the learner's badge and opens the Friend Finder | Tap the central play button | Retrieve name/badge context | Intro started |
| 2. The mission | Three blank profile cards pop out of the device | Tap the three missing picture tokens: name, age, heart | Preview the information goal without relying on reading | All three tokens found |
| 3. Question rehearsal | Keelan models one question at a time with large icon chunks | Arrange `What / do you like / doing?` and hear it | Notice question order and rhythm | Question built correctly |
| 4. Choose a friend | Three children glow in the scene | Choose any child | Learner agency and visual scanning | Friend selected |
| 5. Ask | Keelan leans back and listens | Record **What do you like doing?** | First controlled speech trigger | Meaning matched or support fallback used |
| 6. Listen and remember | The child answers with name, age, interest, and one ability over two short turns | Tap the matching picture on the profile card | Listen for specific information | Required details selected |
| 7. Explore freely | The remaining friends stay available | Meet the other two in either order; choose/replay questions | Repetition with choice | Three profiles filled |
| 8. Find a match | Keelan asks which friend the learner would like to meet again | Choose a friend and a shared/different interest | Compare self with another person | Choice saved |
| 9. Two-turn talk | Chosen friend asks **What do you like doing?** | Record a personal answer; hear the friend respond | Reciprocal listening and speaking | Meaningful answer captured |
| 10. Introduce a friend | Keelan presents a mini stage and the completed profile | Say or assemble **This is ... . He/She likes ... .** | Third-person transfer | Introduction completed |
| 11. Picture check-up | Three random profile clues appear one at a time | Answer 3 listening/visual questions | Retrieval and formative check | 2/3 correct, with retry for missed item |
| 12. Reward and reflection | Friend Finder fills with stars; Keelan celebrates | Choose: “I can ask,” “I can listen,” or “I need another try” | Learner self-assessment | Reflection saved |

### Suggested friend profiles

Use globally accessible names and clear visual differences. The exact names can change during art review.

| Friend | Age | Interest | Ability | Visual anchor |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| Mia | 9 | painting animals | draw a cat | orange paint mark on apron |
| Leo | 10 | playing football | kick with both feet | green ball badge |

## Image production plan

### Visual direction

- Keep the warm, detailed Welcome Fair world, but move to a quieter **Friend Finder corner** so the learning targets are not lost in the crowd.
- Keelan remains full colour at all times. Dim the scene only through an overlay with a cut-out around the current child or object.
- Do not bake labels or dialogue into generated art; all instructional text stays in the interface for accessibility and editing.
- Preserve a central mobile-safe action area and keep important faces above the bottom dialogue panel.

### Build rather than generate

Profile cards, name/age/heart/ability tokens, focus rings, speech bubbles, answer buttons, stars, and question chunks should be interface components or SVG icons. That keeps them sharp, localisable, and easy to bind to learner data.

### Art acceptance checks

- Each friend is identifiable at phone width before highlighting.
- No hotspot overlaps another person.
- Scene reads clearly under a 45–55% dim overlay.
- Keelan has an opaque alpha channel with no dark rectangle or transparency wash.
- Desktop and mobile crops show the same clothing, props, and character features.
- Character proportions and Keelan's blue/orange organic markings match the approved model.

## Audio production plan

### Voice direction

- **Keelan:** retain the approved Coral “young and goofy v2” protocol—bright, playful, short phrasing, slight comic curiosity, never babyish.
- **Friends:** use three clearly different, natural peer-like performances. Keep delivery warm and unhurried; avoid exaggerated accents. If three reliable voices are not available, launch with one featured friend and add the others only after voice review.
- All visible dialogue requires captions and replay. No meaning should depend on sound alone.

### Clip inventory estimate

| Group | New clips | Notes |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Entry and mission | 5 | Welcome back, badge recognition, missing profiles, mission, choose a friend |
| Question models | 8 | Four questions at normal pace plus four chunked/slow models |
| Friend conversations | 15 | Five short clips per friend; separate details so replay can target one item |
| Learner speaking prompts | 5 | Ask, answer, introduce, retry invitation, support fallback |
| Adaptive hints/reactions | 10 | Clear match, unclear question chunk, missing hobby word, listening retry, profile completion |
| Check-up and ending | 7 | Intro, three generic item leads, success, reflection, completion |
| **Estimated new dialogue** | **50** | Final count may drop by reusing generic feedback safely |

Reuse Session 1 tap, correct, retry, sparkle, curtain/spiral, and reward effects. Add only two new effects: a soft Friend Finder scan and a three-star completion flourish. Effects must sit under speech, not compete with it.

### Recording and feedback plan

1. The app asks for microphone access only when the first speaking action is chosen and remembers readiness on that device.
2. The learner records locally and can listen back before choosing **Check my speaking**.
3. Only that explicit check uploads the temporary clip for transcription; raw learner audio is not retained by the lesson.
4. Feedback first confirms the meaning heard, then highlights a specific chunk such as **What do you like** or the key hobby word.
5. A retry never says “wrong.” It offers a slow model, a visual mouth/rhythm cue where useful, and one more attempt.
6. After three attempts, the learner can use supported playback-and-repeat and continue without losing progress.

### Speech-trigger MVP definitions

| Prompt | Model | Match mode | Feedback focus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ask about an interest | What do you like doing? | required question chunks with tolerant filler handling | `what`, `do you like`, `doing` |
| Give a personal answer | I like painting. | meaning + hobby keyword; open vocabulary allowed | `I like` plus intelligible activity |
| Introduce a friend | This is Mia. She likes painting. | meaning + selected profile facts | name, pronoun, `likes`, activity |

The first trigger is the production gate. The other two can begin as transcription-backed feedback without blocking completion.

## Measurement and saved progress

Save the following separately so teachers can distinguish knowledge from microphone or confidence issues:

- friend visit order;
- profile detail accuracy and replay count;
- question-builder attempts and hint use;
- speaking transcript, matched chunks, retry count, and support fallback (not raw audio);
- personal answer concept and whether a reason was attempted;
- final check-up score by language target;
- learner reflection and selected next step.

## Production sequence

1. Approve the Session 2 language scope, friend profiles, and story beats.
2. Generate and review the hero scene and mobile crop before making secondary art.
3. Draft the complete dialogue manifest, then do the user's final script edit.
4. Generate a small voice test for each new friend and approve voice casting.
5. Build the reusable Session 2 data model and scene shell by adapting the Session 1 template.
6. Add question builder, profile listening, and the first speech trigger.
7. Add the supporting practice activities one at a time.
8. Run phone/tablet/desktop play-throughs, including microphone denied, transcription unavailable, and no Session 1 save.
9. Pilot with learners and tune language, idle hints, and clip pacing before marking the session published.

## Decisions to lock before art generation

- Confirm the three friend names and visual anchors.
- Confirm whether all three friends speak in the first pilot or whether one is the voiced MVP.
- Confirm the core answer frame: **I like ...** only, or **I like ... because ...** as the default.
- Confirm whether Session 2 should reuse the learner's Session 1 badge across devices when signed in; the pilot can use local continuity first.
