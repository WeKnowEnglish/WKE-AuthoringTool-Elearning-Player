# Keelan — Session 1 dialogue draft

Status: **Approved for the Session 1 pilot and batch generation.**

Session: Grade 4, Unit 1, Session 1 — Enter the Welcome Fair
Voice: Coral, using the approved `young-goofy-v2` voice protocol
Learning level: Cambridge Movers preparation; clear, short, speaking-first English

## Recording principles

- Keelan sounds like a playful learning companion, not a teacher reading instructions.
- Keep each clip short so the learner can act immediately.
- Use small reactions such as “Ooh!”, “Whoa!”, “Hmm...” and “Boop!” sparingly.
- Never say that an answer or pronunciation is wrong.
- Do not read buttons, timers, transcripts, scores, or every piece of helper text aloud.
- A pronunciation cue means “Keelan was not sure what he heard,” not a phoneme-level diagnosis.

## 1. Opening and explorer badge

### `s1-welcome`

**When:** The learner taps Start adventure.
**Keelan:** “Hi! I’m Keelan. Oh! I’m really glad you’re here. Ready for a new-friend adventure?”
**Direction:** Delighted surprise on “Oh!”; inviting, not rushed.

### `s1-badge-question`

**When:** Keelan points to the blank badge.
**Keelan:** “First things first—what’s your name? Write it on your explorer badge.”
**Direction:** Curious and encouraging; slight emphasis on “your name”.

### `s1-badge-ready`

**When:** The learner finishes writing and wears the badge.
**Keelan:** “Ooh, brilliant! Your explorer badge is ready. Wear it proudly!”
**Direction:** A small celebratory lift; “proudly” should sound warm, not formal.

## 2. Entering and exploring the fair

### `s1-fair-open`

**When:** The full fair is revealed.
**Keelan:** “Ta-da! The fair is open. What do you see first? Tap a glowing station.”
**Direction:** Playful reveal on “Ta-da!” followed by genuine curiosity.

### `s1-explore-mission`

**When:** The learner begins the free-order station activity.
**Keelan:** “There are five activity stations. Explore where ever you want. At each one, tell me: do you like it?”
**Direction:** Make “where ever” sound freeing and game-like.

### `s1-station-sports`

**When:** The learner opens the Sports station.
**Keelan:** “Whoa, football! This boy likes playing football. Can you spot his black-and-white ball?”
**Direction:** Excited opening; clear contrast in “black-and-white”.

### `s1-station-art`

**When:** The learner opens the Art station.
**Keelan:** “Ooh, look at all those colours! She likes painting. What would you paint?”
**Direction:** Wonder at the colours; curious final question.

### `s1-station-books`

**When:** The learner opens the Books station.
**Keelan:** “Aha, the book corner! These friends enjoy reading and sharing their favourite stories.”
**Direction:** Cosy excitement; do not overemphasise “favourite”.

### `s1-station-pets`

**When:** The learner opens the Pets station.
**Keelan:** “Eek—tiny animals! This child has got a pet picture. Which animal can you see?”
**Direction:** “Eek” is delighted, never frightened; small goofy laugh in the voice.

### `s1-station-music`

**When:** The learner opens the Music station.
**Keelan:** “Listen! They can play music together. One plays the keyboard, and one plays the guitar.”
**Direction:** Light rhythmic delivery; clear pause between the instruments.

### `s1-opinion-like-a`

**When:** The learner chooses I like it.
**Keelan:** “Me too! Boop—that goes on your badge.”
**Direction:** Quick, playful reward; make “Boop” a tiny sound effect.

### `s1-opinion-like-b`

**When:** A second positive reaction is needed.
**Keelan:** “Good choice! That one gets a sparkle.”
**Direction:** Short and cheerful.

### `s1-opinion-not-for-me-a`

**When:** The learner chooses Not for me.
**Keelan:** “That’s okay! Everyone likes different things.”
**Direction:** Warm acceptance with no disappointment.

### `s1-opinion-not-for-me-b`

**When:** A second accepting reaction is needed.
**Keelan:** “Fair enough! Let’s discover something else.”
**Direction:** Light and forward-moving.

### `s1-all-stations-complete`

**When:** All five stations have an opinion.
**Keelan:** “You visited every station! High five—well, imaginary high five!”
**Direction:** Goofy pause before “imaginary high five”.

## 3. Personal station choice and first speaking turn

### `s1-choose-station`

**When:** The learner reaches the personal-choice step.
**Keelan:** “Now choose your favourite station. Which one would you like to visit?”
**Direction:** Curious and spacious; leave time after the question.

### `s1-explain-choice`

**When:** The learner selects a station.
**Keelan:** “Great choice! Tell me why you chose it. You can start with: I’d like to visit... because...”
**Direction:** Model the sentence frame slowly and clearly, without sounding corrective.

### `s1-microphone-first-use`

**When:** The learner is about to use the microphone for the first time.
**Keelan:** “Tap the microphone when you’re ready. If your browser asks, tap Allow so I can listen.”
**Direction:** Calm and practical; keep “Allow” clear because it matches the browser action.

### `s1-recording-ready`

**When:** A recording finishes.
**Keelan:** “Got it! Listen back. You can keep it, or try again.”
**Direction:** Reassuring; both choices should sound equally acceptable.

### `s1-check-speaking-invite`

**When:** A recording is ready for transcription feedback.
**Keelan:** “Want my help? Tap Check my speaking, and I’ll listen for the important parts.”
**Direction:** Helpful, never test-like.

### `s1-choice-feedback-clear`

**When:** Keelan heard the station choice and a reason clearly.
**Keelan:** “I heard you clearly! You told me your choice and your reason. Nice speaking!”
**Direction:** Specific praise, lively but sincere.

### `s1-choice-feedback-missing-reason`

**When:** The transcript contains the choice but no clear reason.
**Keelan:** “Nice start! I heard your choice. Now add a reason with because.”
**Direction:** Treat this as a power-up, not an error.

### `s1-choice-feedback-station-unclear`

**When:** The chosen station was not heard clearly.
**Keelan:** “Good start! Say the station name once more, a little slowly.”
**Direction:** Gentle request; no disappointment.

### `s1-feedback-word-unclear`

**When:** Transcription confidence is low for a highlighted word.
**Keelan:** “Good speaking! Try the glowing word once more, a little slowly and clearly.”
**Direction:** Supportive curiosity; “glowing word” connects to the visual cue.

### `s1-feedback-could-not-hear`

**When:** Too little speech was captured.
**Keelan:** “Oops—my ears missed that one! Move a little closer, and let’s try again.”
**Direction:** Make the problem Keelan’s ears, not the learner’s ability.

### `s1-feedback-service-resting`

**When:** Transcription is temporarily unavailable.
**Keelan:** “Hmm... my listening gadget is taking a nap. You can still play your recording and keep going.”
**Direction:** Goofy reassurance; never imply the learner caused the problem.

### `s1-microphone-help`

**When:** Microphone permission is denied or recording is unavailable.
**Keelan:** “Oops! I can’t hear the microphone yet. Ask a grown-up for help, or try again.”
**Direction:** Calm, brief, and non-alarming.

## 4. Picture power-up

### `s1-picture-check-intro`

**When:** The three-question check begins.
**Keelan:** “Three quick picture clues! Can you collect all three power-ups?”
**Direction:** Game-show energy without shouting.

Only three of the following five question clips play in a session.

### `s1-picture-question-sports`

**Keelan:** “Look at the sports station. He likes... playing football, painting, or reading?”
**Direction:** Pause after “likes”; keep all choices neutral.

### `s1-picture-question-art`

**Keelan:** “Look at the art station. She likes... playing football, painting, or reading?”
**Direction:** Pause after “likes”; do not reveal the answer through emphasis.

### `s1-picture-question-books`

**Keelan:** “Look at the book corner. They enjoy... reading, playing music, or making badges?”
**Direction:** Even emphasis across the three choices.

### `s1-picture-question-pets`

**Keelan:** “Look near the animal table. This child has got... a pet picture, a guitar, or a football?”
**Direction:** Pause after “got”; even emphasis across choices.

### `s1-picture-question-music`

**Keelan:** “Look at the music station. They can... play music, read together, or paint a picture?”
**Direction:** Pause after “can”; even emphasis across choices.

### `s1-picture-correct-a`

**Keelan:** “Yes! Power-up collected!”
**Direction:** Fast celebratory burst.

### `s1-picture-correct-b`

**Keelan:** “You got it! Zap!”
**Direction:** “Zap” is a playful sound effect.

### `s1-picture-correct-c`

**Keelan:** “That’s it! Nice looking.”
**Direction:** Praise observation, not intelligence.

### `s1-picture-hint-sports`

**Keelan:** “Almost! Look for the black-and-white ball.”
**Direction:** Helpful clue, never a correction.

### `s1-picture-hint-art`

**Keelan:** “Hmm... look at the paintbrush and all those colours.”
**Direction:** Thoughtful, collaborative tone.

### `s1-picture-hint-books`

**Keelan:** “Take another look at what they’re holding.”
**Direction:** Gentle and curious.

### `s1-picture-hint-pets`

**Keelan:** “Look closely near the animal table.”
**Direction:** Short and encouraging.

### `s1-picture-hint-music`

**Keelan:** “Can you spot the keyboard and guitar?”
**Direction:** Invite discovery rather than giving the answer.

### `s1-picture-check-complete`

**Keelan:** “Three out of three! Picture power-up complete!”
**Direction:** Strong celebration followed by a clean finish.

## 5. Baseline speaking sample

### `s1-baseline-intro`

**When:** The learner reaches the longer speaking sample.
**Keelan:** “Now let’s make your first speaking sample. Tell me your name, your age, and one thing you like. This is a starting point, not a test.”
**Direction:** Slow and reassuring; warm emphasis on “not a test”.

### `s1-baseline-model`

**When:** The learner asks for help or a model.
**Keelan:** “You can say: My name is... I’m... years old... and I like...”
**Direction:** Clear chunks with generous pauses for rehearsal.

### `s1-baseline-feedback-clear`

**When:** All three meaning parts were heard.
**Keelan:** “I heard your name, your age, and something you like. Brilliant!”
**Direction:** Specific celebration.

### `s1-baseline-feedback-name`

**When:** The name introduction was not heard.
**Keelan:** “Nice start! I’m still listening for your name.”
**Direction:** Soft and encouraging.

### `s1-baseline-feedback-age`

**When:** The age was not heard.
**Keelan:** “Nice start! Now add your age.”
**Direction:** Simple next action.

### `s1-baseline-feedback-like`

**When:** An interest was not heard.
**Keelan:** “Nice start! Tell me one thing you like.”
**Direction:** Curious, not corrective.

### `s1-baseline-feedback-several`

**When:** More than one meaning part was not heard.
**Keelan:** “Nice start! Check the three picture clues, then try once more when you’re ready.”
**Direction:** Point the learner toward visual scaffolds.

## 6. Reflection and completion

### `s1-reflection`

**When:** The learner reaches the reflection screen.
**Keelan:** “How did speaking feel today? Pick the answer that feels true for you. Then choose one speaking power-up for next time.”
**Direction:** Calm and sincere; no option should sound preferred.

### `s1-complete`

**When:** Session 1 is complete.
**Keelan:** “Adventure complete! Your explorer badge has its first sparkle. You spoke, listened, and learned about new friends. Great work!”
**Direction:** Proud celebration; finish warmly rather than loudly.

### `s1-practice-invite`

**When:** The learner can continue to the supporting activities.
**Keelan:** “Ready for the next challenge? Take your badge into the practice games!”
**Direction:** Fresh energy that invites, rather than pressures, the learner onward.

## On-screen only — do not generate

- Button labels such as Continue, Record again, Check my speaking, and Power up.
- Microphone timer and readiness status.
- The learner’s transcript.
- “Keelan heard...” evidence chips and highlighted clarity words.
- Reflection answer labels and next-step choices.
- Progress counters such as 3/3 or 5/5.
- Privacy, authentication, or technical configuration messages intended for adults.

## Final review questions

1. Does Keelan sound like the same character from beginning to end?
2. Are any clips too teacher-like, wordy, or babyish?
3. Does every spoken instruction lead to one obvious learner action?
4. Are praise lines specific without overpraising?
5. Are retry lines emotionally safe and useful?
6. Are all Cambridge Movers target structures easy to hear?
