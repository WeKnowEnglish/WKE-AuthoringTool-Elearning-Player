# WKE Educational Comic Series - Master Production Bible

**Version:** 0.1  
**Updated:** 2026-08-02  
**Audience:** Writers, illustrators, AI-asset creators, editors, curriculum reviewers, and lesson-player administrators  
**Learners:** Ages 9-12, CEFR A1-A2

## 1. Purpose and authority

This bible protects story continuity, character identity, visual consistency, age appropriateness, and educational quality across every chapter. It is the first reference used before writing, generating, editing, approving, or uploading a comic page.

The comic should first succeed as an engaging adventure. Language learning is embedded through understandable dialogue, visual context, repetition, and meaningful choices rather than turning every panel into an exercise.

### Canon hierarchy

1. Explicit decisions confirmed by the project owner.
2. Approved final artwork and published dialogue.
3. The newest approved chapter or page script.
4. Working production standards in this bible.
5. Unapproved drafts and AI generations.

If a lower source conflicts with a higher source, stop production and resolve the conflict before creating later pages.

## 2. Series promise

Four friends discover Keelan, a stranded blue alien, and are drawn into a larger mystery. Their adventures combine curiosity, courage, friendship, science, discovery, and age-appropriate wonder.

### Desired learner experience

- Students understand the main action without needing to translate every word.
- Dialogue feels natural, short, and emotionally clear.
- Images provide enough context to infer unfamiliar language.
- Each chapter creates a reason to continue reading.
- Characters model curiosity, cooperation, empathy, and careful decision-making.
- Teachers can use the story for prediction, comprehension, speaking, vocabulary, and retelling.

## 4. Learner and language framework

### Confirmed audience

- **Age:** 9-12 years old.
- **English level:** CEFR A1-A2.
- **Reading context:** Independent reading, teacher-supported reading, paired reading, or lesson-player use.

### Working language standards

- Prefer high-frequency, concrete words supported by the picture.
- Keep most speech balloons between 3 and 10 words; use longer lines only when the meaning remains obvious.
- Keep narration boxes concise, normally 8-18 words.
- Introduce approximately 6-10 useful target words or phrases per chapter.
- Repeat important language naturally across later panels and chapters.
- Use one main communicative function per scene: reassuring, asking, suggesting, predicting, describing, or deciding.
- Use contractions when they sound natural and are easy to read.
- Avoid unexplained idioms, sarcasm, dense exposition, and abstract vocabulary.
- Make the speaker unmistakable through balloon tails, character position, or script notes.
- Let images carry setting and action so dialogue does not describe what students can already see.

### Useful language already established

- Reassurance: “I’m not going to hurt you.”
- Ability: “I can use a little magic.”
- Suggestion: “Maybe we should help him.”
- Imperatives: “Wait!” “Run!” “Stay together.”
- Prediction and possibility: “Maybe it powers the ship.”
- Past event and present result: “My ship crashed. I’m weak.”

## 5. Visual production standard

### Page and panel format

- **Working page standard:** 1122 x 1402 pixels, portrait, approximately 4:5.
- Use 3-6 clearly separated panels per page.
- Use consistent black panel borders and gutters.
- Use parchment-style panel numbers in the upper-left corner when panels are numbered.
- Keep speech text large enough for tablet and classroom display.
- Keep balloon tails clear and directed at the speaker.
- Reserve yellow, black-outlined display lettering for major sound effects.
- Do not place essential text too close to a trim edge.

The completed assets currently contain a format conflict: Chapter 1 pages 3-6 are 1024 x 1536 while the cover, Chapter 1 pages 1-2, and Chapter 2 page 1 are 1122 x 1402. Resolve this before print export or a unified reader presentation.

### Art direction

- Warm, detailed, painterly comic rendering with manga/anime influence.
- Saturated but natural color palette with golden forest light.
- Expressive faces and readable body language.
- Rural valley environment with fields, mountains, village homes, flowers, forest, and a pagoda-like landmark.
- Child proportions and clothing must remain age-appropriate for 9-12-year-old characters.
- Maintain the color-symbol system: Mia/purple, Zara/yellow-sun, Leo/blue-wave, Ethan/green-mountain, Keelan/blue-orange-stars.

### Preferred lettering workflow

Where practical, generate clean artwork without final dialogue, then add balloons, tails, panel numbers, and approved text in a controlled lettering pass. This prevents spelling drift, unclear speakers, and inconsistent typography.

## 6. Environment and object continuity

### Crash-site map

The [fixed crash-site scene map](references/visual/fixed-crash-site-scene-map.svg) is production canon v1.1. The saucer remains west/left, the root/tree hollow east/right, and the ship entrance faces east. Each child keeps their own color-coded backpack; no duplicate loose backpacks remain at the entrance. Keelan begins at the hollow threshold facing west; the children begin between the ship and Keelan facing east.

The default camera stays south of the west-east story axis so the ship reads screen-left and Keelan/hollow read screen-right. Cross the axis only after an establishing panel or clearly shown movement reorients the reader. Chapter 2 page 1 may be retained as approved story art, but its geography must not override this map.

### Spacecraft anchors

- Broad, flattened circular saucer with a raised central dome.
- Dark segmented metal shell.
- Blue illuminated details.
- Round entrance.
- Interior contains a glowing blue crystal or power object.
- Crash damage and smoke may change over time, but the silhouette and doorway must remain recognizable.

### Recurring props

- Mia: purple backpack and science book.
- Zara: yellow/orange backpack and space book.
- Leo: blue backpack.
- Ethan: green backpack.
- Each backpack remains with its owner unless a page explicitly shows that child removing or moving it.
- A front-facing or cropped view may obscure a worn backpack; never add a duplicate loose pack to compensate.

## 7. Chapter architecture

Each chapter should contain a small, satisfying story movement while advancing the larger mystery.

1. **Hook or continuation:** Reconnect readers to the unresolved question.
2. **Immediate goal:** Give the characters a clear problem or decision.
3. **Discovery:** Reveal useful information about Keelan, the ship, the world, or a character.
4. **Choice or complication:** Require cooperation, courage, or reasoning.
5. **Small payoff:** Resolve one question or complete one action.
6. **Forward pull:** End with a new question, discovery, risk, or promise.

### Educational backward-design fields

Every chapter outline must identify:

- Story goal.
- Emotional or character goal.
- Communicative language goal.
- Target vocabulary or phrases.
- Grammar students will encounter, not necessarily study explicitly.
- Visual supports for meaning.
- Three to five post-reading comprehension or speaking prompts.

## 8. Production workflow

1. **Define the learning and story outcomes.** Decide what changes for the characters and what language learners will understand or use.
2. **Write the chapter beat sheet.** Confirm beginning, discovery, decision, payoff, and cliffhanger.
3. **Write the page scripts.** Record panel composition, action, speaker, exact dialogue, emotion, props, background, and continuity anchors.
4. **Run a language review.** Check A1-A2 readability, visual support, dialogue length, and speaker clarity.
5. **Run a canon review.** Compare names, colors, clothing, character models, ship, location, props, and prior page ending.
6. **Generate or illustrate the artwork.** Use the approved prompt blocks and visual references.
7. **Letter the page.** Use only approved script text; verify spelling and balloon tails.
8. **Run page QA.** Complete the continuity checklist at full resolution.
9. **Approve and archive.** Keep one canonical file, place rejected alternatives in `alternates`, and update the chapter record.
10. **Upload to the lesson player.** Confirm page order, thumbnail, mobile readability, and published status.

## 9. File and naming standard

Use lowercase, zero-padded, stable names:

- Chapter folder: `chapter-02`
- Canonical page: `page-01.png`
- Alternate: `alternates/page-01-v1.png`
- Script: `chapter-02-page-01-script.md`
- Prompt record: `chapter-02-page-01-prompt.md`
- Approval record: `chapter-02-page-01-approval.md`

Never overwrite an approved alternate without preserving it. Only one file should be designated canonical for each page number.
