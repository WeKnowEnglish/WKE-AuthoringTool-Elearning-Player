## Cross-chapter issues

### Mia’s hair clip

The v1 model locks exactly one purple clip on Mia’s right side.

- Page 1, panel 3 appears to show two purple clips, one on each side.
- Page 2, panel 3 places the clip on the opposite side.
- Page 6, panel 1 places the clip on the opposite side; panel 3 returns it to the locked side.

**Required correction:** Patch these panels so every visible clip follows the v1 model. Do not change Mia’s hairstyle or add a second clip.

### Character introductions

Leo and Ethan are named in dialogue, but Mia and Zara are never named on-page. Their identities are clear to the production team but not to a first-time learner.

**Recommended correction:** Add a short “Meet the Friends” lesson-player card before the cover, or naturally use “Mia” and “Zara” in revised dialogue. A separate card is preferred because it avoids awkward exposition and preserves the existing story rhythm.

### Page format

| Asset | Dimensions |
|---|---:|
| Cover | 1122 × 1402 |
| Page 1 | 1122 × 1402 |
| Page 2 | 1122 × 1402 |
| Pages 3-6 | 1024 × 1536 |

**Required correction before unified export:** Choose one final reader/print format. Do not crop pages 3-6 automatically; their 2:3 composition is materially taller than the working 4:5 standard. Either recompose them into the approved template or make the lesson player fit both sizes inside one stable page frame without cropping.

### Safety and responsible decision-making

The children run toward a crash, enter an unknown craft, and approach a hidden creature without contacting an adult. “Stay together,” “Slow down,” and “Be careful” provide some protective language, but the behavior should not be presented as real-world safety guidance.

**Recommended educational treatment:** Preserve the adventure, then ask: “What was brave? What was unsafe? What should you do if you find something dangerous?” A later chapter should show the children deciding which trusted adult can help.

## Chapter 1 learning plan

### Communicative goal

Students express an opinion and make a simple prediction about something unknown.

### Measurable learning objective

By the end of the chapter lesson, students can retell four key events in order and use at least one opinion frame and one prediction frame with visual support.

### Success criteria

- I can say who believes in aliens and who does not.
- I can use “I think…” or “I don’t think…” to give an opinion.
- I can use “Maybe…” or “It’s going to…” to make a prediction.
- I can put the meadow, crash, ship, hollow, and Keelan events in order.

### Target language

alien; planet; ship; crash; woods; pilot; move; wait; stay together; impossible

### Reusable frames

- “I think…” / “I don’t think…”
- “Do you think…?”
- “Maybe it…” / “Maybe it’s…”
- “It’s going to…”
- “We have to…”

### Visual supports

- Books support **space**, **planet**, and **alien**.
- The flight path, smoke, and impact support **going to crash** and **crashed**.
- The hollow, rustle, eyes, and fangs support **move**, **pilot**, and prediction with **maybe**.
- Faces support surprise, fear, skepticism, curiosity, and hope.

### Retrieval and speaking prompts

1. What happened first, next, then, and finally?
2. Who believes in aliens? Who does not?
3. What do you think the blue crystal does?
4. Which choices were brave? Which choices were unsafe?
5. What should the children say after Keelan says “Wait”?

### Differentiation

- **A1 support:** Use picture-order cards, point-and-say vocabulary, and complete sentence frames with one missing word.
- **A2 extension:** Ask students to explain opinions with **because**, compare Mia and Ethan, and justify which action was safest.
