# A1-A2 Comic Language Guide

## Learner profile

- Ages 9-12.
- CEFR A1-A2.
- Students may understand more spoken English than they can independently read.
- Visual context should make the main meaning available even when a few words are new.

## Dialogue rules

- Prefer one idea per speech balloon.
- Aim for 3-10 words in most balloons.
- Use common verbs such as go, come, see, look, help, wait, run, find, need, want, know, think, and can.
- Repeat useful sentence frames across different situations.
- Use names when speaker identity or pronouns may be unclear.
- Use punctuation to support expression, but avoid excessive ellipses and exclamation marks.
- Keep jokes understandable from the image or immediate context.

## Narration rules

- Use narration only when the image cannot efficiently show time, location, or a necessary transition.
- Keep narration normally between 8 and 18 words.
- Prefer chronological connectors: after, before, then, next, a moment later, and finally.

## Chapter language plan

Select:

1. One communicative goal.
2. Six to ten target words or phrases.
3. One or two grammar patterns students will repeatedly encounter.
4. At least three visual clues supporting new language.
5. A short post-reading retrieval activity.

## Readability review

- Can an A1 learner understand the central action from the images?
- Can an A2 learner read most dialogue independently?
- Are unfamiliar words inferable from action, expression, or objects?
- Does each pronoun have an obvious referent?
- Does every balloon have a clear speaker?
- Can students retell the page in simple English?

## Recommended post-reading prompts

- What happened first, next, and last?
- How does each character feel? What can you see?
- What do you think will happen next?
- What should the characters do? Why?
- Choose one useful line and use it in a new situation.
