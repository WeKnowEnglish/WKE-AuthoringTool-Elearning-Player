# Upper Primary 1 · Unit 1 exemplar — Who Am I?

Teacher edition · authored 10 October 2026 · use with the [unit-builder guide](./unit-builder-guide.md).

## Unit brief

**Learners:** ages 9–11; A1 consolidation toward A2-oriented communication. Verify actual skill/literacy needs with the entry check. **Purpose:** Help a new classmate discover a shared interest or activity through a useful learner profile and interview.

**Choices:** Use personal, local or fictional details; choose the interest, strength, activity and profile design. No family resources or personal disclosure are required.

**Entry:** Use the original welcome objectives. Also sample familiar hobby meanings, a routine question and simple word decoding; route particular gaps to support while retaining the core path.

**Path:** 9 core × 45 minutes = 405 minutes. Two conditional support workshops are 25 minutes each; one optional extension is 30 minutes. These are teaching estimates; select support from evidence and adjust pacing. The live map shows all 12 labelled slots because conditional routing is manual.

**Preservation:** the saved welcome lesson, its IDs, objectives and targets remain unchanged. L1 timings below are suggested facilitation notes around that existing brief. Existing Unit 1 umbrella objectives and the three reusable lesson IDs remain; more specific focus objectives and new lessons develop the sketch.

**Final task:** help a classmate find something to do with you using an individual profile and interview. Collect fresh listening and reading evidence separately; keep a revised written profile and observe each learner’s own speaking.

**Next-unit bridge:** Use a school-day card: I read stories at school. Art: 9:00; Reading: 10:00. Ask learners to locate Reading at ten and say a familiar school routine. Record subject/clock gaps for U2 L1; teach a missing phrase briefly. Do not require earlier/later/than yet.

## Objectives and source alignment

The existing three unit objectives remain the umbrella goals: understand profiles; exchange interests/strengths/routines; create a learner profile. The six focus objectives below operationalise the dedicated lessons. Original local objectives are aligned to the [CEFR Companion Volume](https://rm.coe.int/16809ea0d4); timings and counts are WKE benchmarks, not official CEFR thresholds.

### UP1.U01.V — Retrieve and use familiar interest and strength chunks for a learner profile.

Learner goal: I can remember useful words for my interests and strengths.

Evidence: recall. Check: Retrieve 6 of 7 selected hobby/strength chunks from meaning or picture cues, then use 2 in relevant personal or fictional statements; record cue support.

Alignment: Sustained monologue: describing experience (pp. 62–63).

### UP1.U01.L — Find useful identity, interest, strength and frequency information in a short spoken profile.

Learner goal: I can listen to a profile and find useful details.

Evidence: recognition. Check: Identify 4 of 5 details in a fresh spoken profile, including an interest and frequency detail; use a fact to select a suitable shared activity. Record replays and visuals.

Alignment: Overall oral comprehension (pp. 48).

### UP1.U01.G — Interpret and use present-simple routine statements, frequency expressions and do questions.

Learner goal: I can say how often I do things and ask about routines.

Evidence: production. Check: Interpret 4 of 5 meaning contrasts; communicate 3 relevant routine statements with 2 frequency expressions and ask one understandable do question. Record model use.

Alignment: Information exchange (pp. 78–79); Sustained monologue: describing experience (pp. 62–63).

### UP1.U01.R — Use information from a short written learner profile to make a relevant choice.

Learner goal: I can read a profile and choose an activity that fits.

Evidence: recognition. Check: Find 4 of 5 requested details in a fresh written profile; support a suitable activity choice with 2 text facts. Rereading is allowed; record glossary support.

Alignment: Overall reading comprehension (pp. 54); Reading for orientation (pp. 55–56).

### UP1.U01.S — Interview a peer about interests, strengths and routines, taking both roles and checking meaning.

Learner goal: I can ask, answer and check information with a partner.

Evidence: production. Check: Complete 4 relevant question-answer exchanges in both roles, communicate 2 interests/strengths and 2 frequency expressions, and check or clarify a detail when needed.

Alignment: Information exchange (pp. 78–79); Asking for clarification (pp. 89–90).

### UP1.U01.W — Plan, write and revise an individual learner profile for a classmate.

Learner goal: I can write a useful profile and improve it for my reader.

Evidence: production. Check: Write 6–8 linked sentences including an interest, a strength and 3 frequency expressions; improve 2 features after feedback. Record frames, word bank or scribing used.

Alignment: Overall written production (pp. 66); Creative writing (pp. 67); Coherence and cohesion (pp. 140–141).

## Core language and sources

Seven interest/strength chunks are the first productive selection. The five frequency words are heard with supported meaning in L3 and explicitly taught in L4. Replace familiar hobby examples when appropriate, retaining the learning purpose and accessible language.

| Chunk | Meaning | Example | Teaching note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| drawing pictures | Making pictures with a pencil or colours. | I like drawing pictures. | Routine form: I draw pictures. |
| reading stories | Reading about characters and events. | I usually read stories after school. | Interest form: I like reading stories. |
| playing football | Playing a team game by kicking a ball. | I sometimes play football. | Interest form: I like playing football. |
| making models | Making a small version of something, such as a house. | I like making models. | Routine form: I make models. |
| solving puzzles | Finding the answers to a puzzle or problem. | I am good at solving puzzles. | Routine form: I solve puzzles. |
| helping a friend | Doing something useful for a friend. | I like helping a friend. | Routine form: I help a friend. |
| good at | Being able to do an activity well. | I am good at drawing pictures. | Teach as a chunk: be good at + activity-ing. Do not rank classmates. |
| always | Every time in the situation being described. | I always read before bed. | Introduce in grammar L4; position before the action verb in these models. |
| usually | Most times in the situation being described. | I usually draw after school. | Introduce in grammar L4; no fixed percentage is required. |
| often | Many times in the situation being described. | I often solve puzzles. | Introduce in grammar L4; distinguish many times from every time. |
| sometimes | On some occasions, but not every time. | I sometimes make models. | Introduce in grammar L4. |
| never | Not at any time in the situation being described. | I never play football before school. | Introduce in grammar L4; I never play, not I do not never play. |

Routine forms: I draw/read/play/make/solve/help. Interest/strength chunks: I like drawing … / I am good at solving … . Model these contrasts without requiring a general gerund rule. Keep third-person -s, new tenses and comparisons outside the core demands.

## Grammar meaning checks and teaching examples

Use everyday context to establish the meanings before asking about form. The example percentages sometimes used on charts are unnecessary.

Practice contrasts: always/every time; never/not at any time; often/many times; sometimes/on some occasions; usually/most times. Teacher models I usually read stories and Do you often read stories? Learners use their own or imaginary routines.

**Fresh meaning check (L4):**

1. “I never play football before school.” Is there a before-school football occasion? (No.)
2. “I always read before bed.” Which meaning fits: every time / some occasions? (Every time.)
3. “I sometimes make models.” Does this mean every time / on some occasions? (Some occasions.)
4. “I usually draw after school.” Which meaning fits: most times / no times? (Most times.)
5. “I often solve puzzles.” Which meaning fits: many times / never? (Many times.)

Then collect three own routine statements using two different frequencies and one do question. Meaning choices alone do not establish productive grammar use. Accept intelligible messages and record scaffolds; give targeted correction after the check.

**S2 changed recheck:** “I never draw before school” (not at any time in that context); “I sometimes read after school” (some occasions); “I always help in this game” (every time in that situation). Ask a new question about making models and two relevant statements.

## Speaking, writing and review materials

Use the student pack for interview cards, a planning grid, the short writing model and revision checklist. ## Adaptation and practical use

- Emerging literacy: point, order or give a brief oral reading/listening response; retain separate writing evidence. Use decoding/phonics support without lowering the communicative purpose.
- Language gaps: use S1/S2 for a defined group with changed-cue rechecks. Other learners apply the same core outcome; support is conditional but necessary for those who need it.
- Greater readiness: fade frames, change the partner or add a relevant follow-up. Keep grammar/vocabulary familiar while independence rises.
- Access: clear print, generous spacing, oral directions and agreed assistive tools. Supply in-class retrieval when home practice is unavailable.
- Parents: “I can tell a classmate about my interests and routines” plus one demonstrated strength, support used and a useful next step. Share individual work only through the teacher’s normal approved workflow.
