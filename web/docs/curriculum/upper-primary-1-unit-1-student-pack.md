# Who Am I? — My learner profile

Use your own details or invent a learner. You can choose what to share. Our goal: help a classmate find something to do with you.

## My interests and strengths

- drawing pictures
- reading stories
- playing football
- making models
- solving puzzles
- helping a friend
- good at

Choose an interest and a strength. Say: “I like …” and “I am good at …”. Routine form: “I draw pictures.” Interest form: “I like drawing pictures.”

## Routine choices

always · usually · often · sometimes · never

I ______ read stories after school. I ______ make models. Do you ______ draw pictures?

Make three true or imaginary routine statements using at least two different frequency words. Ask a partner one do question.

## Read Linh’s profile

Hi! I'm Linh. I'm ten. I like reading stories and making models. I'm good at solving puzzles. After school, I usually read stories. I often draw pictures, but I never play football before school. I sometimes help a friend with a puzzle. Would you like to make a model with me?

1. How old is Linh?
2. Name one activity Linh likes.
3. What is Linh good at?
4. What does Linh usually do after school?
5. What does Linh never do before school?

Choose something to do with Linh. Show two parts of the text that helped you.

## Interview cards

Ask and answer. Take both roles and listen to your partner. Use real or imaginary details.

1. What do you like doing?
2. What are you good at?
3. What do you usually do after school?
4. Do you often [draw pictures / read stories / make models]?

Check a detail: “So, you usually …?” Ask for help when needed: “Could you say that again, please?”

## Plan and write my profile

Model for learning:

Hello! My name is Mina. I like drawing pictures and reading stories. I am good at solving puzzles. I usually read after school. I sometimes play football, but I never play before school. Would you like to draw with me?

Plan your own: name/greeting; interest; strength; three routines with frequency; something useful for your reader. You may use a fictional name and details.

Write 6–8 sentences. Use your own plan and agreed word bank. Put away the complete model when you are ready to compose.

Revision checklist:

- Can a reader find an interest and a strength?
- Did I use three frequency expressions with clear meanings?
- Are the sentences understandable and linked where useful?
- Can I improve a detail, word choice, question, capital or ending mark?
- Keep your first draft and show two useful changes.
