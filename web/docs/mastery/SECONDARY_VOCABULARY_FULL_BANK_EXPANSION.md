## Daily Routines batch

All 20 Daily Routines words now have:

- two distinct contextual examples;
- one reusable collocation or grammatical pattern;
- one supported production prompt and model answer;
- one unseen cloze context; and
- a confusion or usage note where it prevents a likely learner error.

High-value distinctions include:

- `wake up` versus `get up`;
- `arrive at` versus `arrive in`;
- the two common senses of `leave`;
- `rest` versus `relax`;
- `hobby` versus `chore`; and
- literal and phrasal-verb patterns for `take out`.

## Personality batch

All 20 Personality words now meet the same enrichment gate. Contexts emphasize observable behavior rather than fixed labels, and prompts encourage reflection, respectful communication, and growth.

High-value distinctions include:

- `polite` versus `kind`;
- `careful` versus `careless`;

- `shy` versus `quiet`;
- `nervous` versus `excited`;

## Feelings and Opinions batch

All 20 words now distinguish a person's emotional state from the quality that causes it, support respectful expression of agreement and disagreement, and connect challenge language to useful learning strategies.

High-value distinctions include:

- `excited` versus `exciting`;
- `bored` versus `boring`;
- `surprised` versus `surprising`;
- `interesting` versus `interested`;
- `opinion` versus `fact`;
- `difficult` versus the broader senses of `hard`; and
- `easy` versus `simple`.

## Food and Health batch

All 20 words now include practical food contexts, countability guidance, and medically safe language. Symptom examples direct students toward responsible adults or health professionals rather than encouraging self-diagnosis or unsupervised medicine use.

## Places and Directions batch

All 20 words now support functional navigation, transport, and place-description language. The batch models `between A and B`, `opposite`, `near`, sequenced directions, and transport safety.

## Technology and Online Life batch

All 20 words now model digital-literacy language: source checking, download/upload direction, privacy, safe passwords, permission before sharing, constructive comments, and reporting unsafe content to platforms and trusted adults.

## Environment batch

## Stories and Past Events batch

All 20 words now support precise classroom instructions, evidence-based explanations, comparison, focused questioning, and clear use of examples and details. Usage notes reinforce high-value forms such as `reply to`, `different from`, `the same as`, and uncountable `information`.

## Migration policy

1. Migrate complete 20-word topics, not scattered individual words.
2. Count a word as enriched only when examples, pattern, prompt, and cloze context all pass structural validation.
3. Keep legacy fallback active until every word in a topic is complete.
4. Do not generate generic filler solely to reach 240/240 coverage.
5. Run bilingual and teacher review before declaring the full bank curriculum-approved.

## Automated gate

The loader tests identify fully migrated topics, assert all 240 enriched items, and verify distinct examples, productive prompts with model answers, and unseen cloze contexts with valid blanks and answers. This prevents partial or structurally weak content from being reported as complete.

## Remaining work

No authoring batches remain. The complete bank still requires bilingual accuracy review and classroom-level teacher review before it is declared curriculum-approved.
