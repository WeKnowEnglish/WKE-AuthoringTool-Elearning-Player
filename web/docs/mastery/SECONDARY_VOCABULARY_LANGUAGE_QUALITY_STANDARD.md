# Secondary Vocabulary Language Quality Standard

**Phase:** Language enrichment Phase 1  
**Status:** Proposed for approval  
**Primary stakeholder:** Grade 7 students  
**Secondary stakeholders:** teachers reviewing vocabulary and administrators monitoring content quality  
**Applies to:** the Grade 7 A2 vocabulary pack, Word Helper, in-helper quiz, and secondary cloze activity

## 1. Purpose

This document defines what “rich, age-appropriate vocabulary knowledge” means in the secondary lesson player. It is the authoring and review contract for later schema, content, UI, and compiler changes.

The current pack is structurally complete: all 240 items have meanings, an example, chunks, a frame, distractors, and spelling support. This standard raises the target from **field coverage** to **learning value**.

A student should progress through four outcomes:

1. **Understand** the core meaning.
2. **Notice** how the word combines with other words and grammar.
3. **Retrieve** the word in a new but supported context.
4. **Use** the word in a personally meaningful sentence or response.

## 2. Non-negotiable principles

1. **Educational usefulness over volume.** Every example, chunk, distractor, and prompt must teach or test something distinct.
2. **A2 clarity with A2+ growth.** Language surrounding the target word should be comprehensible to a Grade 7 A2 learner while introducing a small, controlled amount of stretch.
3. **Contextual variation.** Presentation, quiz, and cloze must not all reuse the same proposition.
4. **Lexical depth.** Knowing a word includes meaning, collocation, grammatical behaviour, and appropriate use—not translation alone.
5. **Coherent discourse.** A cloze labelled as a paragraph must describe one connected situation, event, explanation, dialogue, or opinion.
6. **Fair assessment.** A correct response must depend on vocabulary knowledge, not on eliminating absurd or grammatically impossible distractors.
7. **Productive transfer.** Every word must provide a supported path toward speaking or writing.
8. **Safe, inclusive contexts.** Examples must avoid stereotypes, humiliation, unsafe advice, unnecessary violence, and assumptions about family structure or resources.

## 3. Target language band

### Core band

- Target vocabulary: A2, with selected high-value A2+ uses.
- Supporting language: predominantly A1–A2.
- Typical full example: 7–14 words.
- Typical cloze sentence: 8–16 words.
- Typical paragraph: 45–90 words, depending on support level.
- Sentence structure: usually one main clause plus no more than one accessible dependent clause.

These are review ranges, not mechanical rejection rules. A four-word classroom instruction may be authentic; a longer sentence may be acceptable when its structure and vocabulary remain transparent.

### Controlled stretch

A sentence may contain one unfamiliar but inferable supporting word when:

- the target word remains the main learning focus;
- the meaning is recoverable from context or visual support; and
- the added word is useful across the curriculum.

Do not place two or more likely unfamiliar content words around a new target word.

## 4. Required knowledge package for each word

Each vocabulary item must ultimately provide the following instructional content. Phase 2 will determine the exact schema.

| Element | Minimum standard | Learning purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| English meaning | One concise, learner-friendly definition | Initial comprehension |
| Vietnamese meaning | One accurate translation for the intended sense | Optional first-language scaffold |
| Introductory example | One clear, concrete use | Meaning in context |
| Transfer example | One different situation or speaker | Flexible understanding |
| Additional model | A third example, mini-dialogue, or contrast when useful | Breadth and pragmatic use |
| Common chunks | Three distinct, natural collocations or patterns | Fluent word combinations |
| Usage pattern | At least one grammatical pattern when relevant | Accurate construction |
| Controlled frame | One scaffolded sentence the learner can complete | Supported production |
| New-context cloze | At least one context not shown in presentation | Retrieval and transfer |
| Production prompt | One age-relevant speaking or writing prompt | Personal use |
| Distractors/confusions | Plausible, fair alternatives | Meaningful discrimination |
| Error note | One high-value usage or form warning when relevant | Error prevention |
| Word relationship | A useful family member, synonym, or opposite when relevant | Lexical network building |

“When relevant” is intentional. A forced opposite or artificial word-family item is worse than no item.

## 5. Field-level authoring standards

### 5.1 English meaning

The meaning must:

- define the sense used by the examples;
- use language easier than the target where possible;
- avoid circular definitions;
- distinguish the word from likely confusions;
- include a short category cue for concrete nouns where helpful; and
- avoid packing multiple senses into one definition.

Good: `a piece of schoolwork that a teacher asks you to do`  
Weak: `a task or assignment that is assigned`

If two common A2 senses are instructionally important, they should become explicitly separated senses rather than one vague definition.

### 5.2 Vietnamese meaning

The Vietnamese meaning must:

- match the exact English sense and part of speech;
- avoid listing several translations without guidance;
- preserve phrase meaning rather than translating each word literally; and
- receive review from a proficient Vietnamese-English educator.

Vietnamese is a scaffold. It must not be required to answer the English use-in-context questions.

### 5.3 Examples

Every word needs at least two semantically different full examples.

Examples must:

- make the intended meaning inferable;
- contain the correct grammatical form of the target;
- model a useful collocation or pattern;
- describe an age-relevant context;
- sound natural when spoken;
- differ in situation, speaker, time, or communicative purpose; and
- avoid merely replacing one noun in an otherwise identical sentence.

Across the examples for one word:

- at least one should be concrete or situational;
- at least one should demonstrate a high-value chunk;
- pronouns and subjects should vary naturally;
- affirmative, negative, or question forms may vary when authentic; and
- the target may appear in an inflected form if that form is explicitly supported.

Examples must not expose the exact wording later used in the assessed cloze.

### 5.4 Common chunks and usage patterns

Chunks must be corpus-plausible, useful to Grade 7 learners, and structurally distinct.

Prefer combinations such as:

- verb + noun: `submit an assignment`
- adjective + noun: `healthy meal`
- verb + preposition: `arrive at school`
- fixed frame: `in my opinion`
- noun + preposition: `reason for`

Avoid three superficial variants such as `good project`, `big project`, and `nice project` unless each is genuinely high value.

When a word has a required pattern, record it explicitly. Examples:

- `arrive at + small place` / `arrive in + city or country`
- `advice` is uncountable: `some advice`, not `an advice`
- `interested in + noun/-ing`
- `apologize to someone for something`

At least one displayed example must instantiate each high-priority pattern.

### 5.5 Controlled sentence frame

The controlled frame must:

- support the learner in producing a true or plausible sentence;
- include enough context to constrain the intended use;
- model the correct grammar around the target; and
- differ from the assessed cloze sentence.

Prefer a frame that allows personalization:

`I need to revise ___ before ___.`

Avoid using only a copy of the introductory example with the target blanked.

### 5.6 Production prompt

The prompt must make genuine use possible without requiring sensitive disclosure or specialist knowledge.

It should include:

- a clear communicative goal;
- an optional sentence starter;
- a short model response; and
- enough flexibility for more than one correct answer.

Example:

- Prompt: `What school subject would you like to improve? Why?`
- Starter: `I would like to improve ___ because ___.`
- Model: `I would like to improve science because I enjoy experiments.`

### 5.7 Error and confusion support

Add an error note only when it prevents a likely learner error. It may cover:

- spelling;
- false friends;
- countability;
- prepositions;
- word form;
- confusing pairs; or
- inappropriate register.

The note must be brief and actionable. Prefer a contrastive model:

`advice` (noun): `She gave me advice.`  
`advise` (verb): `She advised me to rest.`

## 6. Word Helper experience standard

The Word Helper should follow this learning sequence:

1. **Meet the word:** word, accurate part of speech, pronunciation/spelling support, and core meaning.
2. **See it work:** introductory example with target highlighting.
3. **Build the pattern:** one or two labelled collocations or grammar patterns.
4. **See a new use:** transfer example or mini-dialogue.
5. **Try it:** controlled frame or personal prompt.
6. **Retrieve it:** short quiz using unseen contexts.

### Display rules

- Do not label chunks as sentences.
- Do not show three chunks when they repeat the same pattern.
- Show the raw linguistic category when useful (`phrasal verb`, `preposition`, `conjunction`) rather than collapsing everything to `phrase` in learner-facing copy.
- Related words and opposites should appear only when their relationship is clear and useful.
- English remains the main interaction language; Vietnamese stays revealable on demand.

## 7. In-helper quiz standard

### Required assessment balance

A three-question run should normally contain:

1. one meaning question;
2. one new-context use question; and
3. one form, collocation, grammar, or controlled-recall question.

Do not include both definition-to-word and word-to-definition questions in the same three-question run unless no stronger question is available.

### Context novelty

- Assessed sentences must not duplicate a displayed example or frame.
- Replays should rotate contexts, not only shuffle the same choices.
- The target collocation or grammar clue may recur, but the proposition should change.

### Distractor rules

Every distractor must:

- be grammatically compatible with the prompt unless grammar is the tested distinction;
- match the target’s part of speech or construction;
- be semantically plausible at first glance;
- be clearly wrong when the full context is considered;
- remain within a similar familiarity and difficulty range; and
- avoid creating two defensible answers.

Reject distractors that are unrelated enough to make the answer obvious, such as pairing a health word with `keyboard` in a simple health sentence.

### Feedback rules

- Correct feedback should reinforce one meaning, collocation, or grammar clue.
- First-error feedback should direct attention without revealing the answer.
- Final feedback should give the answer and one brief explanation.
- Do not repeat the complete dictionary definition after every correct response.

Example first-error feedback: `Look at the words after the blank: we usually say “arrive at school.”`

### Retrieval progression

| Mastery state | Appropriate support |
| --- | --- |
| New | 3 choices, strong context, optional Vietnamese reveal outside assessment |
| Learning | 4 plausible choices, unseen context |
| Secure | typed word or collocation completion |
| Review due | unseen context with reduced hints |

## 8. Cloze language standard

### Activity naming

- Use **paragraph cloze** only for connected discourse.
- Use **complete the sentences** when items are independent.
- Never create the appearance of cohesion by adding sequence markers to unrelated sentences.

### Coherent passage requirements

A paragraph cloze must have:

- one clear topic and situation;
- a stable speaker, viewpoint, time frame, and register;
- logical reference between sentences;
- connectives chosen for meaning, not position;
- 3–5 target blanks where possible; and
- enough non-blank context for each answer to be inferred.

Appropriate discourse families include:

- school-day narrative;
- student dialogue;
- advice exchange;
- online message or post;
- opinion paragraph;
- past-event story;
- directions exchange;
- project reflection; and
- short factual explanation.

### Blank rules

Each blank must:

- test the intended word or supported form;
- have one clearly best answer;
- preserve natural grammar after insertion;
- avoid relying only on a/an or singular/plural as the clue;
- avoid removing so much text that the meaning disappears; and
- remain scorable against explicitly accepted forms.

Do not place two blanks so close together that neither can be solved independently unless the activity intentionally tests a fixed phrase.

### Cloze distractors

Distractors should be topic-related and grammar-compatible. A distractor is valuable when the learner must read the whole sentence or passage to reject it.

The bank should normally contain:

- all correct answers;
- 1–3 plausible distractors depending on support level; and
- no duplicate forms unless the UI and scoring support repeated use.

### Support progression

| Level | Design |
| --- | --- |
| Supported | Full word bank, strong contextual clues, optional meaning hint |
| Developing | Plausible distractors, less direct clues, same-topic passage |
| Mastery | Reduced/no bank, typed response, or required inflected form |

## 9. Content differentiation

Difficulty should represent the language task, not merely word length.

| Level | Expected demand |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Concrete meaning; highly familiar context; direct collocation |
| 2 | Standard A2 context; one useful pattern; recognition and supported recall |
| 3 | Less concrete meaning, polysemy, or more demanding collocation; transfer context |
| 4 | A2+ productive use, confusion contrast, or grammar-sensitive retrieval |
| 5 | Reserved for later B1 bridge work; not required for the initial G7 pack |

The current pack may continue to label all target entries A2, but internal difficulty must meaningfully distribute language demand across levels 1–4.

## 10. Automated quality gates

Later implementation should produce blocking errors and review warnings.

### Blocking errors

- missing required meaning or introductory example;
- target absent from a full example without a declared inflected-form match;
- ambiguous cloze with multiple accepted-looking answers;
- cloze answer does not produce grammatical text;
- exact duplicate assessed context and displayed example;
- duplicate word/item identifiers;
- unsafe or age-inappropriate content; or
- Vietnamese meaning does not match the authored sense after human review.

### Review warnings

- introductory example outside the target length range;
- examples with near-identical propositions;
- three chunks sharing the same shallow pattern;
- unrelated distractor;
- distractor part-of-speech mismatch;
- definition containing the target or a trivial derivative;
- forced or misleading opposite;
- paragraph with inconsistent tense, speaker, or situation;
- unexplained supporting vocabulary above the target band; or
- production prompt permitting only one memorized answer.

Automated checks support human review; they do not certify naturalness by themselves.

## 11. Human review rubric

Each item receives 0–2 points in six dimensions.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning precision | inaccurate/unclear | understandable but broad | precise and learner-friendly |
| Context quality | unnatural or unhelpful | clear but generic | natural, inferable, age-relevant |
| Context variation | repeated proposition | minor variation | genuinely different uses |
| Collocation/grammar | absent or misleading | useful but incomplete | high-value pattern clearly modelled |
| Assessment fairness | obvious/ambiguous | usable | plausible, grammatical, diagnostic |
| Productive support | none | frame only | frame plus flexible personal use |

### Approval threshold

- No dimension may score 0.
- Total score must be at least 10/12.
- Meaning precision and assessment fairness must each score 2.
- Vietnamese meaning must pass bilingual review.

## 12. Twenty-word pilot acceptance criteria

Phase 2/3 should begin with one complete School Life topic containing 20 words.

The pilot is ready for UI and activity work when:

- 20/20 words meet the required knowledge package;
- 20/20 have at least two distinct full examples;
- 20/20 have a controlled frame and an unseen cloze context;
- 20/20 have at least one useful collocation or grammatical pattern instantiated in an example;
- 20/20 have a production prompt with a model;
- 100% of quiz distractors pass grammar and ambiguity review;
- the topic supports at least two coherent passage families;
- all items score at least 10/12 on the human rubric;
- a Vietnamese-English educator approves all translations; and
- student testing shows that learners can explain or use the target in a new context, not merely recognize the displayed sentence.

## 13. Pilot evaluation

Test the pilot with representative Grade 7 learners and record:

- first-attempt meaning accuracy;
- unseen-context accuracy;
- typed or spoken production success;
- distractors frequently chosen;
- requests for Vietnamese support;
- time per question;
- examples or instructions learners find confusing; and
- teacher judgments of naturalness and teachability.

The purpose is not to maximize immediate scores. A richer question may lower first-attempt accuracy while providing better evidence of learning.

## 14. Governance and source of truth

- The versioned Grade 7 JSON remains the content source of truth until the Phase 2 schema is approved.
- The Lesson Player mastery engine remains the runtime mastery source of truth.
- Language enrichment must not create a parallel vocabulary bank or mastery store.
- Every pack release must record a content version, reviewer, date, and change summary.
- Material language changes require content QA even when TypeScript and schema tests pass.
